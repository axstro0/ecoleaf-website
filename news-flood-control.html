What the flood control scandal means for private construction

Ecoleaf TeamEcoleaf Development Corporation · November 2026

When the flood control scandal broke in 2025, most of the headlines were about public money: ghost projects, substandard dikes and the officials and contractors accused of profiting from them. For those of us in private construction, it has also been a hard look in the mirror. The public now asks a question every builder should be ready to answer: can we trust the people who build our country?

What happened

Government records showed about ₱545 billion spent on 9,855 flood control projects from July 2022 to May 2025, with roughly a fifth of that amount going to just 15 contractors. Investigations by the Senate, the House and the Independent Commission for Infrastructure (ICI), created in September 2025, uncovered projects that were overpriced, unfinished or never built at all. The Philippine Contractors Accreditation Board (PCAB) revoked the licences of nine companies linked to one contractor family, and the government ordered the blacklisting of contractors behind defective projects.

The ripple effect on private construction

The damage did not stay in the public sector. As audits and reviews slowed government infrastructure spending, the wider economy felt it. In its mid-2026 Philippine Economic Update, the World Bank projected growth of 3.7% for 2026, down from 4.4% in 2025, partly because of delayed public infrastructure spending.

For private builders, the effects show up in three ways:

  • Clients ask harder questions. Developers, retailers and institutions now look closely at a contractor's licence, track record and finances before they sign. Being cheapest is no longer enough.
  • Trust has become a deliverable. A clean record, transparent billing and honest progress reports now carry as much weight as price and schedule.
  • The whole industry pays for a few. Every contractor, good or bad, now works under the same cloud of suspicion. The only way out is to prove, project by project, that most of us build honestly.

Why this is a turning point

Scandals like this can either harden public cynicism or raise the standard for everyone. We believe it should be the second. Stricter accreditation, open records and real consequences for wrongdoing are good for honest builders. They reward the companies that already invest in proper engineering, licensed people and quality materials.

A building is a promise that outlives its contract. A flood wall that fails, or a column that cuts corners, puts lives at risk long after the ribbon is cut. That is why integrity in construction is not a slogan. It is a safety requirement.

What clients should look for

  • A valid PCAB license in the right category for the size of the project
  • SEC registration and proper bonding and insurance
  • A portfolio of completed projects you can visit, and clients you can call
  • Clear contracts, itemized costs and regular, documented progress reports

Where Ecoleaf stands

At Ecoleaf, we are registered with PCAB and the SEC, and we are bonded. More importantly, we measure ourselves by the same thing our clients do: buildings that are finished on time, built to specification and safe for the people who use them.

The flood control scandal has cost the country a great deal. If it leaves behind an industry where honesty is expected and verified, not just promised, then something good can still come from it. We intend to be part of that.
